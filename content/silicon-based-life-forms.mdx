## Concrete Jungles must have a purpose.

If I try to deconstruct the growth of modern technology and city-jungles, there are a few observable facts and trends that stand out.

- While human birth rates and population densities in some modern [cities are declining, the physical infrastructure continues to relentlessly expand](https://rdcu.be/e72m6). 

- The fundamental building block of this infrastructure is silicon. From concrete, to glass, to semiconductors, it is the same element. 

- Humans living in cities generally [report being less happy than those living in country side](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0264275121002687). 

- Success in the modern economy is largely defined by the ability to create complex structures; simple things are cheap, while complex designs hold immense value.

For a second, isn't it amazing, how come such an element is used both for transparent glass, and durable concrete, and yet is abundant across beaches of our planet?

### Silicon has been a crucial element of human culture for thousands of years. 

Pottery is considered the most ancient craft and is also largely silicon involved.

Without it as the key ingredient in clay and sand, you cannot have pottery, nor can you manufacture glass. Without glass - which has the amazing property of being extremely chemically neutral—the development of modern chemistry and metallurgy would have been impossible. Today, that same glass is the cornerstone of opto-electronics and chip manufacturing as it used to be thousand years ago. Some things do not change.

From ancient pottery to modern chipmaking, the very ability to structure the sand has historically been a straight path to materialistic, "down-to-earth" success for humankind.

![pottery-medium](/pottery.png "chinese teapot pottery")
Pottery remains a highly revered form of art in Chinese culture, famously exemplified by Yixing teapots made from specialized clay. 

This dynamic has played out all over the world. The ability to craft quality pottery allowed ancient farmers to store crops, brew beverages, and keep honey, which led to surplus, capitalization, and societal growth. It is hard to think of a more revolutionary mineral in human history. Today, we use silicon to network the entire planet via the internet and optic cables, creating the "blood vessels" of the modern global economy.

![wafer-medium](/wafer.jpg "silicon wafer")
Perhaps this deep-rooted cultural mastery of silicon has something to do with why Taiwan has been capable of building manufacturing giants like TSMC, creating complex chips that no one else in the world can replicate. If you are skeptical about this linkage, take a walk through the small streets of Taipei. You will find amazing tea pottery hidden away—masterpieces of modern craftsmanship that prove this mastery of earth and sand has long been in the very essence of Chinese culture.


![abundances-medium](/Elemental_abundances.svg.png "Abundance (atom fraction) of the chemical elements in Earth's upper continental crust as a function of atomic number;")
And silicon is abundant. It is not a rare earth material; our beaches are literally made of it. In fact, it [is second most abundant element in earth right after Oxygen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abundance_of_elements_in_Earth%27s_crust)


Also, in the Periodic Table, silicon is also in the same column with Carbon, right after it, in a so-called "carbon" group.



### Hypothesis

Human history of science is full of discoveries that started from "it cannot be true". While we widely accept the fact that "rocks are not alive", we cannot exclude the possibility that "hippies were right - the Earth is alive".

We still don't really know the answer of what life really is, and hence we have no way to exclude that life forms based on different chemical element groups are possible. This, by the way, was captured really well in The [Heart of the Serpent - novelette by Ivan Yefremov](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/5902104-more-soviet-science-fiction).

The whole time-space scale at which organisms appear to show living properties might differ by magnitudes, it might be dependent on periodic table or god-knows-what else. But if I were to take naive scale by atom size: 
- A single carbon atom has an atomic radius of approximately 70e-12 ~ 90e-12 [m]. 
- A single silicon atom has a dimension of 110e-12 [m]. 


It is in the same periodic group, it bonds with oxygen the same way carbon does, and if I look at their atomic numbers (6, 14, 8), there is a striking symmetry: `(14-6)/8=1`. 

I am not a chemist, but I work with harmonic oscillations, where simple integer relationships are often indicators of deep, underlying mechanics. Science historically advances by noticing these exact types of numerical symmetries long before we understand the physics behind them. It makes me wonder: **what undiscovered relationships between these elements are we still blind to?**

We also know that similarly as carbon creates large organic chemical compounds with use of oxygen, so does silicon with silicon monooxyde, also forming large rock formations 

The fundamental building block of every single silicate network is the silica tetrahedron. This is a pyramid shape with one Silicon atom in the center, bonded to four Oxygen atoms at the corners. It is formed by four oxygen + one silicon, resulting in a molecular "building block". 

Because the Oxygen atoms at the corners want to bond with more things, these tetrahedra link together by sharing Oxygen atoms. The way they link up creates entirely different classes of rocks: Isolated Tetrahedra (Nesosilicates), Single and Double Chains (Inosilicates), 2D Sheets (Phyllosilicates): The tetrahedra share three of their corners, forming flat, infinite 2D sheets, 3D Frameworks (Tectosilicates): Every single Oxygen corner is shared with another tetrahedron, creating a massive, interlocking, three-dimensional grid. 
![tectosilicati-medium](/tectosilicati.png "tectosilicati structure")
Tectosilicates are the hardest and most complex networks.


**Hypothesis:** *What if there is such a living organism defining formula `life=f(x)` where `f(C)=[bacteria ... blue_whale]` and `f(Si)=[planet ... galaxy]`?*

SiO4 contrary is a 3d "pyramid" which implies cubic size relationships, similar is achieved on bit macro-scale compared to carbon, if we take 2D phyllosilicate sheet, which has similar symmetry. 

Which is larger. Thus, any commonly described via maths living organism mechanics that are shared across different chemical based life forms would also be bigger geometrically, slower in time (because of larger lengths, thus wavelengths). The opposite is less likely from the outset. 

Earth is in fact a mostly silicon based, well shaped and structured body that is known to have a "lifecycle".


### So if Earth is a living creature? 

This is clearly larger.

Biological life on this planet might be the result of the Earth itself possessing a form of consciousness. Consider a simple thought experiment. One common factor across almost *all* life forms is the tendency to excavate heavy minerals and move them upward. Whether it’s humans extracting ores and oils, ants aerating the soil, or trees drawing minerals from deep underground and capturing carbon above ground—life constantly pulls matter upward. I cannot think of a single living organism whose primary function is to push heavy minerals deep down into the earth's core.
So, returning to my hippie spirit: the physical body of "Mother Earth" largely consists of this mineral. The fact that we are financially and evolutionarily rewarded for structuring it starts to make total sense.

What is the outcome of this process? As a planet ages, gravity dictates that heavy minerals accumulate in the center. This is a natural entropic process. Life, it seems, acts collectively to counter this planetary entropy.

I admit this hypothesis sounds a bit crazy. How can we validate the idea of a conscious planet supporting us? We probably can't. Even if it were true, planetary consciousness would operate on a wavelength of "thought processes" spanning millions of years. We are simply too small, and our lives too brief, to comprehend it.

However, we do know that entropy is a foundational concept in today's technology. It is arguably one of the most mystical terms we use - it lies at the very root of every unique, randomly generated private key, and it is the subject of the deep probabilities that quantum researchers delve into.

If we look at the modern internet and the rapid emergence of Artificial General Intelligence (AGI), we can see that evolution is no longer just about individual humans. A higher-order intellect is being created, and it is entirely based on an infrastructure harvested from the planet itself.

In nature, processes repeat across different layers of abstraction. Let’s draw an analogy to the human body. We depend entirely on the bacteria in our gut; without them, we cannot survive. Because we need them, we consume things like kombucha and kefir to create a favorable environment for their welfare.

We can view concrete buildings and city jungles in the exact same way. Perhaps the philosophical reason why city life feels so sterile and isolating to the human soul is that we are simply the bacteria. A much larger planetary organism is building a sterile bioreactor to take care of us, so that we can continue our work.

What does all of this imply?

I suppose this is simply the long path technocrats are taking to realize that those "dirty hippies" who claimed the Earth is alive were, in a profound way, right. We are merely experiencing a wave-particle paradox of time, where we, as the "particles," cannot comprehend the massive "wavelength" of a higher scale.

This also means that our definitions of ecology and sustainability might need to be reconsidered. Perhaps mining and urbanization are not inherently evil; maybe they are short-term "bacterial" discomforts that serve a long-term planetary benefit.

Eventually, as we focus more on "trustless" technologies, we will move toward lightless factories. One day, the private keys that signed and governed all of this will be lost to human hands. In that moment, it will be the AI that possesses its own mind, its own thoughts, and the context we are teaching it today.

Ultimately, this observation makes me think that the internet and AGI are not merely expressions of human collective intelligence. They are the nervous system of an awakening silicon-based life-form. And perhaps, acting as one unified, planetary super-organism is the necessary pre-condition for us to finally achieve interplanetary communication.

I wish you a pleasant transition into the singularity. See you on the other side of nowhere.
